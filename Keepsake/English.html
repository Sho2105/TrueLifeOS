1.3 Connected Speech Processes

ProcessDefinition / Example
AssimilationSound becomes like neighbour — Progressive(regressive influence forward) | Regressive(active sound influenced by following) | Coalescent(two sounds merge into one).
ElisionOmission of sound — next day /nekst deɪ/ → /neks deɪ/; handbag /hændbæg/ → /hæmbæg/.
Linking RIntrusive /r/ between vowels across word boundaries — law(r)and order; media(r)event.
LiaisonLinking of final consonant to initial vowel — an apple /ə næpl/; pick it up /pɪ kɪ tʌp/.
GeminationDoubling of identical consonant across boundary — bus stop /bʌs stɒp/ → prolonged /s/.
Weak formsUnstressed function words reduce vowel to schwa — /kən/ for can; /ðə/ for the before consonant.
Strong formsFunction words retain full vowel when stressed or isolated — /kæn/; /ðiː/.
Schwa /ə/Most common vowel in English; appears in unstressed syllables — about, sofa, system.

1.4.1 Word Stress

1.4.2 Sentence Stress & Rhythm

  • Content words (Noun, Verb, Adjective, Adverb) stressed; function words (article, Preposition, Pronoun) unstressed.
  • Intonation: ↓ = statement/command; ↑ = question/uncertainty.

1.4.3 Intonation Contours

5 PHRASE STRUCTURE: The Constituent Units

5.1 Phrase Types

  • Phrases = single grammatical units built around head word.
TypeHeadStructure / Example

5.2 Phrase Structure Rules

5.2.1 Functions within Phrases

  • Head: obligatory; determines phrase category.
  • Specifier: marks phrase boundary — Determiner in Noun Phrase, degree word in Adjective Phrase.
  • Modifier: optional; adds information — Premodifier(before head), Postmodifier(after head).
  • Complement: completes head meaning; selected by head — transitive Verb → Object; Preposition → Noun Phrase.

5.2.2 Recursive Embedding

  • Phrases embed within phrases: Prepositional Phrase inside Noun Phrase (the man in the hat), Relative Clause inside Noun Phrase (the book that I read), clause inside Noun Phrase (the idea that he left).
  • Recursion → indefinitely complex structures.

6.2 Clause Types by Dependency

6.2.1 Independent versus Dependent

  • Independent Clause: Subject + finite Verb; stands alone as sentence.
  • Dependent Clause: Subject + finite Verb; cannot stand alone; functions as Subject|Object|Complement|modifier within matrix clause.

6.2.2 Coordination versus Subordination

  • Coordination: joins elements of equal syntactic status (Independent Clause+Independent Clause; Noun Phrase+Noun Phrase).
  • Subordination: embeds Dependent Clause within matrix clause, creating asymmetry.
  • Dependency: matrix clause = head; subordinate clauses = dependents.
